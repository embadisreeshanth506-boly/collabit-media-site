Turn an idea into a focused product brief

An app idea becomes easier to evaluate when you can describe who it is for, the task it helps them complete, and why the existing alternatives fall short. Begin with one core journey rather than a long list of disconnected features.

What is an MVP?

A minimum viable product is a focused first version that helps test an important assumption with real users. It is not simply a complete product with unfinished details. Decide which outcome the first release must support and what can be deferred.

For example, a new scheduling tool might begin with creating and confirming a booking. Advanced reporting and multiple permission levels may belong in a later version if they are not needed to test the central idea. This is a planning example, not a claim about our portfolio.

Choose a prototype, web app, or mobile app deliberately

An interactive prototype can help review a user journey before investing in a working product. A web app runs through a browser; a mobile app may be appropriate when device capabilities or a dedicated mobile experience are central to the idea.

The right approach depends on users, distribution, integrations, budget, and maintenance. Scope authentication, payments, data storage, and third-party services explicitly rather than treating them as small visual features.

Plan what happens after the first release

Write down the behaviour that would show whether the product is useful. That might be completion of a core task or feedback from a defined group of users. Plan a feedback process and prioritise the next iteration from evidence.

Questions before you begin

What affects the cost of an MVP?

The core workflow, number of user roles, integrations, data requirements, and quality checks all affect scope. A clear first-release brief is more useful than a feature list without priorities.

Should I build every feature before launch?

Not necessarily. Separate essential functions from later improvements. The first release should be coherent and usable for its intended task, with clear acceptance criteria.

Can development and launch marketing be planned together?

Yes. Align the product audience, positioning, website message, and launch content early. Agree the exact development and marketing deliverables in your project scope.
