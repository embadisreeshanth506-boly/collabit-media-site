A practical starting point

Before you build,
write a better brief.

A website and app project brief checklist for founders and small businesses. Clear inputs make proposals easier to compare and reduce avoidable misunderstandings.

1. Define the outcome and the audience

Write one sentence describing the person you want to help and the action they should be able to complete. “Help prospective customers request a service quote” is more actionable than “make a modern website.” For an app, name the core task rather than listing every possible screen.

2. Separate essentials from later ideas

List the pages or user journeys needed for that outcome. Mark each item as essential for launch or a possible later improvement. Include payment, login, booking, and data requirements explicitly. These can change the scope more than the number of visual screens.

Read about MVP product development if you need to prioritise a first app release, or website development if your primary need is a business presence.

3. Prepare content and evidence

Collect your logo, service descriptions, approved photographs, product screenshots, and customer quotes you have permission to publish. Identify who will write and approve the content. Label illustrative concepts clearly and avoid making outcome claims without evidence.

4. Describe the practical constraints

  • Budget: provide a working range and identify recurring costs separately.
  • Timing: explain whether a date is a preference or tied to a specific event.
  • Ownership: agree who controls the domain, hosting account, code, and design files.
  • Maintenance: decide who updates content and handles future changes.
  • Communication: name the decision-maker, time zone, and review process.

5. Plan the launch and measurement

Choose the first audience and how they will find the site or app. Make sure a real enquiry route works before promoting it. Agree what you will measure, such as completed enquiries or a key product action, and who will review the results.

A digital marketing plan should connect your offer, content, distribution, and measurement. More traffic is useful only when it reaches the right people and they can take the next step.

A short brief you can adapt

“We help [audience] achieve [outcome]. We need [website or app] so users can [core action]. Launch must include [essentials]. We already have [assets]. Our budget range is [range], our preferred launch date is [date], and approvals will come from [decision-maker].”
